\section{Session Lifecycle and \texttt{claim-forked}}
\label{sec:lifecycle}
% ===================================================================


\texttt{spec-opencode-integration.tex} models how a session is
\emph{correlated} with a delegation. It says nothing about how a session
roster comes to exist, how it persists or is reclaimed, or how a forked
child acquires a delegation by a sequence of file writes that a process
may abandon at any point. Those are the subject of
\fpath{docs/design-opencode-plugin.md} (DES-078): \S2.1.1 (\field{ensure},
the two sidecars, \field{isStale}), \S2.3 and \S2.3.1 (the ancestor walk),
\S2.6 (\field{session delete}), and \S5 points~1 and~2 (\field{Store.Ensure}
and \field{claim-forked}). That prose has been through three rounds of
review (rounds 5, 6 and 7 of PR \#536), and each round found a
counterexample the previous one had not. The organisation's rule for a
defect class that survives two rounds is to stop reviewing and model it;
this document is that model.

The model is a state machine over the operations \emph{as the design
document currently states them}. It was written first with every
amendment switched off, so that ProB could be asked whether the invariants
of \S\ref{sec:lc-invariants} hold of the prose as it stood. They did not.
\S\ref{sec:lc-ledger} records each counterexample, the amendment that
removes it, and the switch that turns that amendment on. The operations
below, with every switch on, are the authority the design document is to
be brought into line with; with a switch off, the corresponding operation
is the prose of rounds 6 and 7.

\subsection{Method, and two facts about the toolchain}
\label{sec:lc-method}

\textbf{One machine per file.} \texttt{probcli} reads a \fuzz{} document
as a single B machine: the state is the schema that \field{Init} primes,
and every other schema is promoted to an operation. The Registry machine
of \texttt{spec-opencode-integration.tex} and the machine of this document
are independent, so they live in separate files; an earlier revision
embedded this one in the Registry file and the promotion of its schemas
raised the Registry's transition count from 3862 to 14692 and left its
validation uncertified. This file loads directly in \texttt{fuzz} and
\texttt{probcli}: \field{Init} is the empty system, and the scenario
initialisations \field{LcInitPair} and \field{LcInitChain} are alternatives
for it (\S\ref{sec:lc-init}), selected by the commands of
\S\ref{sec:lc-verification}, as are the slices.

\textbf{Convention for invariants.} Every invariant that is a state
predicate is checked as the negation of that predicate given to ProB as a
\texttt{-goal}, never as a predicate of a state schema. This is stated once
here and applies to every row of \S\ref{sec:lc-verification}. The reason
is the next paragraph.

\textbf{State predicates are guards.} \texttt{probcli}'s Z-to-B
translation puts only the typing of the state schema into the B
\texttt{INVARIANT}; every predicate in the state schema is conjoined to
the post-state of each operation, as a guard. An operation that would
violate such a predicate is not reported as violating it: it is
silently disabled, and the state is simply not reached. A ``zero
invariant violations'' report from \texttt{/z-spec:test} therefore says
nothing about the predicate part of a state schema (the earlier
Validation figures of \texttt{spec-opencode-integration.tex} are of this
kind; see \S\ref{sec:lc-findings}). The state schema of this section accordingly has
\emph{no predicate}. Each invariant is stated as its own schema
(\field{LcInv}\ldots), which \texttt{probcli} never sees, and is checked by
giving ProB its negation as a \texttt{-goal}: a counterexample is then a
reachable state satisfying the goal, and ``no counterexample, all states
visited'' is exhaustive for the instance.

\textbf{Slicing.} The full machine has thirty-four operations over
thirty-three variables; ProB explores about two hundred states a second.
Each invariant is therefore checked on a \emph{slice}: the same schemas
with the operations that cannot affect that invariant removed, and a
scenario \field{Init} where a particular lineage is needed. A removed
operation leaves its variables at their initial values. The slices are
named in \S\ref{sec:lc-verification}.

\subsection{Types and constants}

abstracted to three kinds, because \field{isStale} distinguishes no more:

\subsection{State}
\label{sec:lc-state}

The state is the conjunction of twenty-one small schemas, so that an
operation can name the components it leaves alone with $\Xi$ rather than
by equations. Session level: \field{roster} is the set of existing roster
files, with \field{rkind}, \field{started} and \field{corrupt} (a roster
whose file no longer parses). \field{parentSide} is the
\fpath{parent-session} sidecar; it is a function from a session to its
recorded parent, and may have an entry for a session with no roster.
\field{seen} is the \fpath{last-seen} sidecar and \field{seenBad} the
sessions whose sidecar does not parse. Claim level: \field{claimed} and
\field{corigin} are the active-mission sidecar, \field{binding} the
delegation-binding sidecar, \field{pend} and \field{pendWit} the
\fpath{claim-forked-pending} record (delegation and witness), and
\field{stage} and its companions are a \field{claim-forked} write phase that
is \emph{in flight}, holding the child's dispatch-pending lock (stage $k$
is the next write to perform; \S\ref{sec:lc-ops}). Delegation level:
\field{used} are the ids ever allocated by \field{NewID}, \field{skel} the
skeletons on disk, \field{dOpen} those whose verdict is still open,
\field{dMis} and \field{dOwn} a skeleton's mission and child, and
\field{missOpen} the missions that are open. A skeleton also has a
kind, \field{dKind}: written by \field{claim-forked} (\field{dkFork}) or by a
Tier B dispatch (\field{dkTierB}). \field{dAbort} is the set of closed
skeletons whose verdict is \field{aborted}; any other closed skeleton was
closed by a sweep, with a pass verdict. \field{todo} is the snapshot of an
unlocked sweep that has been taken and not yet written. \field{cfH} is the
set of in-flight \field{claim-forked} calls that hold the locks of step~5
of \S5 point~2 (the shared mission lock and the delegation lock). The
ghosts are \field{sealed}, \field{purgedLive}, \field{leaked},
\field{unlocked}, \field{overwritten} and \field{liveAborted}: they record history so that an invariant over a transition can be
checked as an invariant over a state.

\field{sealed} records, when a roster is written, the parent that was then
recorded for it; \field{LineageStable} says the sidecar never departs from
it while the roster exists. \field{purgedLive} collects every session whose
roster a purge removed while it was demonstrably live. \field{leaked}
collects every child for which a \field{claim-forked} call ended with an
interrupted attempt's skeleton still open and not live.

\field{unlocked} collects every child for which \field{claim-forked}
wrote a binding or a claim without holding the locks of step~5.
\field{overwritten} collects every skeleton whose verdict a transition changed
after it was closed; it is computed, by one rule, by every operation that
writes a verdict, from the \field{dAbort} of the pre- and post-state.
\field{liveAborted} collects every child whose live binding's skeleton a
\field{claim-forked} reconcile closed.

\field{leaked} is a ghost, and it is only worth anything if it is computed
by one rule that does not know which amendment is on. Every operation that
can end a \field{claim-forked} call other than by ``write failed'' (that
is, \field{LcCfRefuse}, \field{LcCfReconcileClaimed}, \field{LcCfExitNoRetry}
and \field{LcCfFinish}) therefore sets it by the same equation, in both
branches of every switch, from the \emph{post}-state:
\[
\field{leaked}' = \field{leaked} \cup \{ c? \mid \exists rd \in \field{pend}(\{c?\}) \bullet
  rd \in \field{dOpen}' \land \lnot\,\field{live}'(c?, rd) \}
\]
where $\field{live}'(c, rd)$ says $c \mapsto rd$ is in \field{binding}$'$ and the
child's claim in \field{claimed}$'$ is for the mission that \field{dMis}$'$
gives $rd$. The record read is the pre-state one, because that is the
attempt being exited; whether its skeleton was closed is read off
\field{dOpen}$'$, which the operation's own predicate determined. A mutant
operation that omits its closing step therefore leaks, and the invariant is
checked of what the operation does and not of what it says it does
(\S\ref{sec:lc-mutants}). An earlier revision set \field{leaked} only in the
branches with the switch off, so \field{ReconcileOnExit} held of the
amended operations by construction; that revision is withdrawn.


\subsection{The invariants}
\label{sec:lc-invariants}

The invariants, each as a schema over \field{Lifecycle}, followed by
the well-formedness conditions that validate the model itself. A schema here is a statement, not a guard (\S\ref{sec:lc-method}).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \field{LineageStable}: while a roster exists, the parent that
    \field{Store.ParentOf} reports is the one that was recorded when the
    roster was written.
  \item \field{Acyclic}: the recorded-parent relation has no cycle. This is
    the strong form. It is stated of every sidecar, not only of those
    reachable from some roster, and it makes the visited set of the
    ancestor walk (\S2.3.1) a defence against a corrupt file rather than a
    necessity; the walk keeps its visited set and refuses on a repeat.
  \item \field{NoLiveSessionPurged}: no purge removes a roster whose
    last-seen is within the TTL, or whose numeric participant is live.
    ``Live'' is judged from what ethos can read: the participant kind of an
    unreadable roster is not evidence.
  \item \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton}: every open fork-propagated skeleton is
    named by a pending record or by the binding of an existing child. This
    literal form is the first conjunct of the stronger property below.
  \item \field{ReconcileOnExit} (second conjunct of the stronger
    \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton}): no \field{claim-forked} call ends, other
    than by ``write failed'', leaving an interrupted attempt's skeleton open
    when it has not adopted it and its binding is not live. The literal form
    holds of a crashed attempt for ever, because the record names the
    skeleton; the harm of an unreconciled attempt is that nothing may ever
    reconcile it (the child may be refused for good), so the postcondition
    is on the exit of the call.
  \item \field{SweepComplete}: an open skeleton whose mission is closed is
    named by a pending record. This is what \field{Store.Close}'s sweep is
    for.
  \item \field{NoForeignAbort}: no skeleton written by a Tier B dispatch is
    ever closed with verdict \field{aborted} by any operation of the model
    (including release and the close-time clearing). The model has one such writer of a
    Tier B skeleton (the dispatch), so the invariant is a predicate over the
    set of aborted skeletons.
  \item \field{NoVerdictOverwrite}: the verdict of a skeleton, once not open,
    never changes. The ghost \field{overwritten} collects every skeleton for
    which a transition changed a closed skeleton's verdict, computed by one
    rule over the pre- and post-state of every operation that writes a verdict.
  \item \field{LockedWrites} (a condition of the model's lock assumption, not
    an invariant of the design): every binding or claim that
    \field{claim-forked} writes is written by a call holding the locks of
    step~5. \field{LcCloseLocked} excludes only calls that hold them.
  \item \field{LiveSpared}: no \field{claim-forked} reconcile closes the
    skeleton of a live binding, that is, one that the child's binding names
    while the child's claim is for the skeleton's mission.
\end{itemize}

\begin{schema}{LcInvLineageStable}
Lifecycle
\where
\forall s : roster @ s \in \dom sealed \land sealed(s) = parentSide \limg \{ s \} \rimg
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvAcyclic}
Lifecycle
\where
\forall s : \dom parentSide @ s \notin (parentSide \plus) \limg \{ s \} \rimg
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvNoLiveSessionPurged}
Lifecycle
\where
purgedLive = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvNoOrphanOpenSkeleton}
Lifecycle
\where
\forall d : dOpen | (d \mapsto dkFork) \in dKind @ (\exists c : \dom pend @ pend(c) = d) \lor (\exists c : roster \cap \dom binding @ binding(c) = d)
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvNoForeignAbort}
Lifecycle
\where
\forall d : dAbort @ (d \mapsto dkTierB) \notin dKind
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvNoVerdictOverwrite}
Lifecycle
\where
overwritten = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvLockedWrites}
Lifecycle
\where
unlocked = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvLiveSpared}
Lifecycle
\where
liveAborted = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvReconcileOnExit}
Lifecycle
\where
leaked = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvSweepComplete}
Lifecycle
\where
\forall d : dOpen | d \notin todo @ (\exists c : \dom pend @ pend(c) = d) \lor (\exists m : missOpen @ (d \mapsto m) \in dMis)
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{LcInvWellFormed}

\where
\dom rkind = roster \\
\dom started = roster \\

\dom corigin = \dom claimed \\
\dom pendWit = \dom pend \\

\dom dMis = skel \\
\dom dOwn = skel \\
\dom dKind = skel \\

\end{schema}

\paragraph{\field{deleteFiles}} (\S2.6; \fpath{store.go}:278--301). The call
stops at the first failure, so a failed call has performed a prefix of its
steps; \field{done?} is the length of that prefix. The steps are numbered
in the order the code performs them. As written, the last-seen and
parent-session sidecars precede the roster file, so step~7 is the roster
removal and a failure there leaves a roster whose lineage sidecar has gone.
With \field{fixDeleteOrder} the roster is removed at step~6 and the
lineage sidecar last, so the sidecar can outlive the roster (which the
design already treats as authoritative) but never the reverse. With
\field{fixDeleteCloses} step~1 closes, with verdict \field{aborted}, every
open skeleton named by the session's pending record or binding, before
either is cleared.

\paragraph{\field{claim-forked}} (\S5 point~2). The read phase takes no lock
and writes nothing; \field{LcCfBegin} is a read phase that passes (the
child's roster loads, the witness holds a claim-origin binding, and the
witness is reached by the walk of \S2.3.1: each hop is read from
\field{parentSide} and the session reached must have a readable roster) and
that goes on to take the child's dispatch-pending lock. \field{LcCfRefuse}
is a read-phase refusal. The write phase reconciles the pending record
first (stage~0), in one of three ways: the child already holds a claim
(\field{LcCfReconcileClaimed}), the record is resumable
(\field{LcCfReconcileReuse}), or it is closed and removed
(\field{LcCfReconcileFresh}). It then allocates an id and re-checks that
the mission is open (stage~2), and writes the record, the skeleton, the
binding and the claim, one stage each (3 to 6), before removing the
record. A process may \field{LcCfCrash} at any stage, leaving the files
written so far; or, if it survives a failed write, \field{LcCfAbort}.

With \field{fixRefuseReconciles} a refusal also reconciles. With
\field{fixInertBinding} the claimed case closes the skeleton unless the
binding is live, that is, unless the child's claim is for the skeleton's
mission. With \field{fixPostCheck} the binding is not written unless the
mission is still open: the check that follows the skeleton write, which
closes the window the unlocked sweep leaves. With \field{fixSupersede} a
reconcile that does not resume the record also closes the skeleton named by
the child's binding when that is a different delegation, because the write
phase is about to overwrite the binding.

\paragraph{Tier B dispatch} (\fpath{pretooluse\_dispatch.go}:775--884).
\field{LcTierBDispatch} is the path Claude Code already runs when a leader
spawns a subagent against a mission. It takes the id (\field{NewID}), the
shared mission lock and the delegation lock, re-reads the mission, and
writes the skeleton with \field{ParentSession} equal to the \emph{session
itself}; it then writes the session's own delegation binding, naming the
new skeleton, over whatever the binding named before. Nothing in the
dispatch closes the skeleton: \fpath{subagent\_stop.go} does not, and
\field{CloseDelegation} has no caller outside the tests, so a Tier B
skeleton stays open until \field{Store.Close} sweeps it
(\fpath{mission/store.go}:1239 skips only a skeleton that is already not
open). The operation is atomic here, since it holds the shared mission
lock; the window in which the unlocked fallback can pass it is that of
\S\ref{sec:lc-limits}. A Tier B skeleton has kind \field{dkTierB}; the
skeleton \field{claim-forked} writes has kind \field{dkFork}. In the code
the kind is the skeleton's \field{BoundVia} (a new value for
\field{claim-forked}); the binding sidecar carries no field of its own and
its origin is that of the skeleton it names.

\subsection{Counterexamples and amendments}
\label{sec:lc-ledger}

Every counterexample below was found by ProB against the operations as
DES-078 rounds 6 and 7 state them, that is, with the switches \field{zfalse}.
This section was first committed in that form (revision \texttt{e47050a},
eight switches); the ninth, \field{fixSupersede}, was added when C9 appeared
with the other eight on, and five more (\field{fixOrigin},
\field{fixReleaseCloses}, \field{fixSessionStart}, \field{fixCas},
\field{fixReuseLocks}) with C10 to C15, which a review of the design
against the Claude Code paths the model had omitted found; C1 to C9 were run
again on the final text, and C9 was found to have been reached by a wrong
model of \field{mission release} (its errata). Each
trace is the shortest breadth-first path to a state satisfying the negated
invariant, in the notation of the operations; ``$a$, $b$'' are sessions,
$m_1$, $m_2$ missions and $d$ a delegation. The first four are the
counterexamples of the three review rounds; they were reproduced, not
assumed. The rest were not known.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{C1}] \textbf{Reconcile skipped on a read-phase refusal.}
    \field{ReconcileOnExit}; \S5 point~2, the order of the read and write
    phases. $b$ is forked from $a$, which holds a claim. \field{LcCfBegin}$(b,a)$;
    \field{LcCfReconcileFresh}; \field{LcCfAlloc}$(b,d)$; \field{LcCfWriteRecord};
    \field{LcCfWriteSkeleton}; \field{LcCfCrash}; \field{LcCfRefuse}$(b)$.
    The record is reconciled only in the write phase (step~2), after the read
    phase has had the chance to refuse; a refusal returns with $d$ open and
    its record in place, and a child that is refused for good never reaches
    the write phase. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixRefuseReconciles}: every exit
    other than ``write failed'' takes the child's dispatch-pending lock first
    and reconciles. The prose of round 7, ``every call reconciles first'',
    was true of the write phase only.
  \item[\textbf{C2}] \textbf{\field{deleteFiles} removes the parent sidecar
    before a roster removal that then fails.} \field{LineageStable};
    \S2.1.1 and \S2.6. \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(a)$; \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(b,\{a\})$;
    \field{LcDeleteSession}$(b,6)$: the first six steps succeed, the seventh, the
    roster file, fails. $b$ has a roster, no recorded parent, and
    \field{sealed}$(b)=\{a\}$: a later \field{ensure} for $b$ with any parent
    other than $a$ is a lineage conflict, and with none it succeeds, so the
    roster's lineage has changed. (With \field{--parent-gone} the same
    happens in two steps, with $a$ its own parent.) \textbf{Amendment}
    \field{fixDeleteOrder}: remove the roster at step~6 and the
    \fpath{parent-session} sidecar last. A failure at step~7 leaves a sidecar
    with no roster, which the design already treats as authoritative and
    \field{session delete} already clears.
  \item[\textbf{C3}] \textbf{Re-registration after deletion closes a cycle.}
    \field{Acyclic}; \S2.1.1 rule~1 and \S2.3.1. \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(a)$;
    \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(b,\{a\})$; \field{LcDeleteSession}$(a,7)$;
    \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(a,\{b\})$. $b$ is registered, so rule~1 is
    satisfied, and nothing refuses an edge that completes $a\to b\to a$. The
    design's remark that a cycle can only be written through
    \field{--parent-gone} is false; it is also true without deletion, as
    $a\to b$ with $b$ unregistered and \field{--parent-gone}, then
    $b\to a$. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixCycleCheck}: when a new edge is
    written, refuse if the session is the supplied parent or an ancestor of
    it by the recorded chain, whether or not the ancestors have rosters.
  \item[\textbf{C4}] \textbf{An operator claim of a different mission leaves
    the skeleton open.} \field{ReconcileOnExit}; \S5 point~2, the round-7
    outcome ``record and binding name the same delegation''. As C1 up to
    \field{LcCfWriteBinding}; \field{LcCfCrash}; the operator claims $m_2$,
    different from the skeleton's mission $m_1$: \field{LcOperatorClaim}$(b,m_2)$;
    a later \field{LcCfBegin}$(b,a)$; \field{LcCfReconcileClaimed}$(b)$. The record
    names the binding's delegation, so the call removes the record only. The
    binding is inert ($m_1\neq m_2$) and the skeleton is open, named by
    nothing that will be read again. The prose accepts this (``stays open only
    until its own mission closes''); \field{countBlockingDelegations} does
    not, since the open skeleton blocks \field{Abandon}. \textbf{Amendment}
    \field{fixInertBinding}: in the claimed case, close the skeleton unless
    the binding is live, that is, unless the child's claim is for the
    skeleton's mission.
  \item[\textbf{C5}] \textbf{\field{deleteFiles} on the child orphans its
    skeleton.} \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton} (literal form). As C1 up to
    \field{LcCfWriteSkeleton}; \field{LcCfCrash}; \field{LcDeleteSession}$(b,7)$. The
    pending record, the only name the skeleton had, is cleared (step~4) and the
    roster removed; the skeleton stays open. The design says so, ``a skeleton
    it named stays open only until its mission closes''. For a binding the
    same happens after a completed write. \textbf{Amendment}
    \field{fixDeleteCloses}: step~1 of \field{deleteFiles} closes, with
    verdict \field{aborted}, every open skeleton named by the session's record
    or binding. It runs under the child's dispatch-pending lock and takes
    the mission and delegation locks in the order of \S5 point~2; a failure
    to close is a failure of the call, with the roster kept as the retry token.
  \item[\textbf{C6}] \textbf{The unlocked sweep leaves a skeleton the sweep
    can never reach.} \field{SweepComplete}; \fpath{store.go}:1107--1114.
    \field{LcCfBegin}$(b,a)$; \field{LcCfReconcileFresh}; \field{LcCfAlloc};
    \field{LcCfWriteRecord}; \field{LcCloseUnlockedBegin}$(m_1)$ (the exclusive lock could
    not be taken, so the sweep ran beside the write phase; its snapshot is
    empty, since the skeleton does not exist yet);
    \field{LcCfWriteSkeleton}; \field{LcCfWriteBinding}; \field{LcCfWriteClaim};
    \field{LcCfFinish}. The mission is closed and its skeleton is open and
    bound. The code comment calls this ``a narrow TOCTOU window''; nothing
    ever closes the skeleton. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixPostCheck}: re-read
    the mission after the skeleton is on disk, before the binding. If the
    mission was closed before the skeleton existed, the check sees it; if
    after, the sweep sees the skeleton. On a closed mission the call aborts
    (\field{LcCfAbort}). This closes the window the locked path does not have.
    It is not enough alone: the aborted write can still be crashed, leaving
    a record, and C5 must be fixed too (\S\ref{sec:lc-verification}).
  \item[\textbf{C7}] \textbf{A live process does not protect a roster that
    has an old last-seen.} \field{NoLiveSessionPurged}; \S2.1.1 table, row~2.
    \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(s,\{p\},\mathrm{touch},\mathrm{numeric live})$;
    \field{LcClockSet}$(2)$; \field{LcPurgeSession}$(s,7)$. The table asserts
    ``stale (idle past the TTL decides, even with a live PID)''. The invariant
    of the mission contract says the opposite, and the row is the
    counterexample. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixStaleLive}: a live numeric
    participant protects the roster whatever the last-seen says. Last-seen
    still decides for a roster that has no live numeric participant, which is
    every OpenCode roster; and a dead numeric participant with a fresh
    last-seen is still not stale. \textbf{Row~2 of the table reverses.}
  \item[\textbf{C8}] \textbf{A roster that does not parse is purged whatever
    its last-seen says.} \field{NoLiveSessionPurged}; \fpath{store.go}:391--397,
    which deletes on a \field{Load} error before \field{isStale} is consulted.
    \field{LcEnsureCreate}$(s,\ldots,\mathrm{touch})$; \field{LcCorruptRoster}$(s)$;
    \field{LcPurgeSession}$(s,7)$. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixCorruptPurge}: read
    the last-seen first, as \field{isStale} does; an unreadable roster with a
    valid last-seen within the TTL is left alone. (A Claude Code roster that
    does not parse and has no last-seen is purged as today.)
  \item[\textbf{C9}] \textbf{A second propagation overwrites the binding of
    the first.} \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton} (literal form), found only
    after C1 to C8 were amended. A completed propagation: \field{LcCfBegin}$(b,a)$;
    reconcile; \field{LcCfAlloc}$(b,d_1)$ and the four writes; \field{LcCfFinish}.
    Then the child's claim is replaced by an operator claim of another
    mission $m_2$ and $m_2$ is closed by the child's own session
    (\field{LcOperatorClaim}, \field{LcCloseLocked}, \field{LcClearClosed}):
    \field{ClearMissionBindings} clears the claim, which names $m_2$, and
    leaves the binding, which names $d_1$ of $m_1$ (the two tests are
    independent, \fpath{mission/active.go}:907--935). A second
    \field{claim-forked}: \field{LcCfBegin}$(b,a)$; reconcile (the record is
    gone); \field{LcCfAlloc}$(b,d_2)$; the writes up to
    \field{LcCfWriteBinding} (run \texttt{C9\_nosup}, nineteen steps). The
    binding now names $d_2$; $d_1$ is open, named by no record and no
    binding. \textbf{Errata (this revision).} The first statement of C9 reached
    the same state by \field{LcReleaseClaim} leaving the binding in place.
    That was a defect of the model, not of the code: \field{ethos mission
    release} clears the binding as well as the claim
    (\fpath{cmd/ethos/mission.go}:2150--2153), so after a release there is no
    binding for a second propagation to overwrite. The state is reached by
    the close-time clearing above, which is why \field{fixSupersede} is
    still needed (\texttt{C9\_nosup} counterexample, \texttt{C9\_all}
    holds). With the durable pending record of C12 the clearing closes the record-named skeleton itself, and the row without \field{fixSupersede} holds (\texttt{C9\_nosup\_dur}); the amendment is then redundant for this invariant. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixSupersede}, with its scope stated
    exactly: it applies on the two reconcile paths that go on to write, that
    is \field{LcCfReconcileFresh} and \field{LcCfReconcileReuse}, whether or
    not a pending record exists, and on no other path. It closes the open
    \field{dkFork} skeleton the child's binding names when that is not the
    skeleton the call will use: on the fresh path every such skeleton, on the
    resume path the one that differs from the record's. It is never applied
    by \field{LcCfRefuse} or \field{LcCfReconcileClaimed}, whose child either
    does not write or holds a claim, and may be working under the binding. The
    earlier wording, ``a reconcile that does not resume the record'',
    contradicted the schema of \field{LcCfReconcileReuse}, which carries the
    clause; it is withdrawn.
  \item[\textbf{C10}] \textbf{\field{deleteFiles} and the reconcile closes
    abort a delegation they do not own.} \field{NoForeignAbort}; found by
    reading the Claude Code path the model had left out
    (\fpath{pretooluse\_dispatch.go}:775--884). A Tier B dispatch writes the
    leader's \emph{own} delegation binding, with \field{ParentSession} equal
    to the session itself, and leaves the skeleton open until
    \field{Store.Close} sweeps it. \field{LcTierBDispatch}$(s,m,d)$;
    \field{LcDeleteSession}$(s,2)$ (run \texttt{F1D\_off}, four steps, three
    states). The step~1 of C5, which closes every open skeleton named by the
    session's binding, aborts $d$: a running subagent's delegation, which the
    leader's session ending (or being purged) does not end. The same happens
    in \field{LcCfReconcileFresh} and \field{LcCfReconcileReuse}, whose
    supersede close (C9) names the child's binding, which a Tier B dispatch by
    the child may have written. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixOrigin}: a
    skeleton has a kind, \field{dkFork} for the one \field{claim-forked}
    writes and \field{dkTierB} for the dispatch; every close of
    \field{deleteFiles}, purge, refusal, claimed case, resume and supersede
    closes only an open skeleton of kind \field{dkFork} whose owner is the
    session. In the code the kind is the skeleton's \field{BoundVia}, which
    \field{claim-forked} sets to a new value; the binding sidecar gains no
    field, since the skeleton it names carries the kind.

    unnames open.} \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton}; found by reading the
    release command that C9 had modelled wrongly. After a completed
    propagation, \field{LcReleaseClaim}$(b)$ clears the claim and the binding
    and nothing closes $d_1$ (run \texttt{Fa\_orph\_norel}: eleven steps). The
    skeleton is open, named by nothing, and the child that held it has no claim.
    \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixReleaseCloses}: \field{mission release} closes,
    \field{aborted}, the open \field{dkFork} skeleton owned by the session that
    its binding names (and, with \field{fixDurableName}, the one its pending
    record names, removing the record), before it clears the binding, under
    the same locks as the other closes. A binding that names a Tier B skeleton is cleared
    and the skeleton is left open: it is a subagent's delegation.
  \item[\textbf{C12}] \textbf{A Tier B dispatch by a propagated child buries
    its fork skeleton.} \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton}. The child $b$ completes
    a propagation (binding $d_1$, kind \field{dkFork}) and then spawns a
    subagent against the mission: the dispatch writes \emph{its} binding for
    $b$ over $d_1$ (\fpath{pretooluse\_dispatch.go}:880--884, unchanged by
    this design), and $d_1$ is open, named by nothing
    (\field{LcTierBDispatch}, run \texttt{C12\_off}, eleven steps). Every close
    of the design is by name, and the binding was the only name a completed
    propagation had, since the pending record was removed at \field{LcCfFinish}.
    \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixDurableName} (the leader's ruling, option
    (c)): the \field{claim-forked-pending} record persists for the life of the
    claim and is the durable name of the fork skeleton. \field{LcCfFinish}
    keeps it, marked complete (\field{pendDone}, the record's stage); it is
    removed only together with the skeleton it names, by \field{mission
    release} (C11), by the close-time clearing of the claim
    (\field{LcClearClosed}) and by \field{deleteFiles}, each of which closes
    the skeleton first (kind \field{dkFork}, owned, compare-and-set on
    verdict \field{open}). Reconcile changes meaning with it, because a record
    now also exists for a propagation that succeeded. A \emph{complete}
    record is never resumable (\field{LcCfReconcileReuse} requires
    $c\notin\field{pendDone}$), and it is live, whatever the binding names,
    when the child's claim is for the skeleton's mission; an incomplete record
    is live only if the binding names it as well, as in C4. In the refusal and
    the claimed case a live record is kept and marked complete, and any other
    is closed and removed. The ghosts \field{leaked} and \field{liveAborted}
    use the same notion of live. With the switch on and no assumption,
    \field{NoOrphanOpenSkeleton} holds including the Tier B over fork case
    (\texttt{C12\_on}).

    fork replaces its roster.} \field{LineageStable}; \S2.1.1 (``a
    \field{SessionStart} for any other source keeps today's
    \field{CreateInCheckout} path'') and \S2.5. A fork is registered through
    \field{Ensure}, which records its parent; the same session is later
    resumed, or compacted, and the hook, which for these sources is

    sidecar that records one. \field{LcSessionStart}$(b,\mathit{fork},\{a\})$;
    \field{LcSessionStart}$(b,\mathit{resume})$ (run \texttt{S\_lin\_off}: with
    the first step an \field{LcEnsureCreate}, two). The roster says root, the
    sidecar says child. The design's remark that \field{Ensure} never
    overwrites a recorded lineage, ``so a repeated or resumed
    \field{SessionStart} cannot erase it'', holds only of the fork source.
    \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixSessionStart}: every source routes through
    \field{Ensure} without \field{--touch}; the sources other than
    \field{fork} supply the parent \field{ParentOf} already records. The
    replacing create is then reached only by the CLI \field{session create},
    which refuses an id whose sidecar records a parent (\field{LcCreateCli},
    unchanged).
  \item[\textbf{C14}] \textbf{The unlocked sweep overwrites the verdict of a
    reconcile close.} \field{NoVerdictOverwrite}; \fpath{mission/store.go}:1107--1114
    and \fpath{mission/delegation.go}:649--692. \field{closeDelegationSkeletons}
    reads a record, finds it open, and calls \field{CloseDelegationSkeleton},
    which loads the record again and rewrites it with the verdict it is
    given, whatever the record now says. When the exclusive mission lock
    cannot be taken the sweep runs beside a reconcile: it reads $d$ open, the
    reconcile closes $d$ \field{aborted}, the sweep writes its own verdict over
    it. The model splits the unlocked fallback into the snapshot
    (\field{LcCloseUnlockedBegin}) and the writes (\field{LcSweepWrite}), one
    skeleton each. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixCas}: every skeleton close,
    the sweep's included, is a compare-and-set on verdict \field{open}, made
    under the delegation lock; the sweep takes the delegation lock of each
    skeleton it closes, after the mission lock if it holds one, which is the
    order of \S5 point~2. A verdict, once not open, never changes.
  \item[\textbf{C15}] \textbf{The resume path writes before it holds the locks
    it is excluded by.} \field{LockedWrites}; \S5 point~2 item~5 step~7. The
    resume path of the reconcile finishes the missing writes. The model took
    it straight to the binding and claim stages, which \field{LcCloseLocked}
    treats as holding the shared mission lock; the code takes the child's
    dispatch-pending lock, reconciles (releasing the locks of its close), and
    only then takes the mission and delegation locks of step~5 and re-reads
    the mission. Between those a locked close may run and sweep the mission.
    \field{LcCfBegin}; \field{LcCfReconcileFresh}; \field{LcCfAlloc};
    \field{LcCfWriteRecord}; \field{LcCfWriteSkeleton}; \field{LcCfCrash};
    \field{LcCfBegin}; \field{LcCfReconcileReuse}; \field{LcCfWriteBinding}
    (run \texttt{Fa\_lockw\_off}): a write of the phase made with the step~5 locks
    not held. \textbf{Amendment} \field{fixReuseLocks}: the resume leaves the
    call at the stage before the allocation; \field{LcCfLockReuse} takes the
    locks and re-reads the mission (and the call exits ``do not retry'' if it
    is closed); only then may the binding and the claim be written. The
    exclusion of \field{LcCloseLocked} is now a set of calls that hold the
    locks, \field{cfH}, and not a stage number.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Verification}
\label{sec:lc-verification}

\paragraph{Commands.} The file loads directly: \texttt{fuzz} type-checks it
and \texttt{probcli} reads it with \field{Init} as the empty system, in which
case the two scenario initialisations are, regrettably, operations that jump
to their scenario state; no claim below rests on such a run. A \emph{slice}
is the same file with the switches set, the invariant schemas, the scenario
initialisations that are not wanted and the operations the slice removes
deleted, and the wanted initialisation renamed \field{Init}. With
\texttt{F} this file, \texttt{INIT} one of \field{Init}, \field{LcInitPair},
\field{LcInitChain}, and \texttt{V$n$} \texttt{ztrue} for each switch to be on
and \texttt{zfalse} otherwise:

\begin{verbatim}
cp F S.tex
for n in fixDeleteOrder fixDeleteCloses fixCycleCheck fixRefuseReconciles \
         fixInertBinding fixStaleLive fixCorruptPurge fixPostCheck fixSupersede \
         fixOrigin fixReleaseCloses fixSessionStart fixCas fixReuseLocks fixDurableName \
         fixResumeClosed; do
  sed -E -i "s/^$n = z(true|false)( [\\\\][\\\\])?\$/$n = V_$n\2/" S.tex
done
sed -i '/^[\\]begin{schema}{LcInv/,/^[\\]end{schema}/d' S.tex
for s in LcInitPair LcInitChain DROPPED_OP ...; do      # all but INIT
  sed -i "/^[\\\\]begin{schema}{$s}\$/,/^[\\\\]end{schema}\$/d" S.tex
done
# INIT other than Init: delete the schema Init, rename INIT to Init
sed -i 's/^\([\\]begin{schema}\){INIT}$/\1{Init}/' S.tex
probcli S.tex -card SESSIONID s -card MISSIONID m -card DELEGATIONID d \
  -p MAX_INITIALISATIONS 1000 -p MAX_OPERATIONS 1000 \
  -goal "GOAL" -mc N -bf -nodead -check_goal
\end{verbatim}

\noindent The switch sed is written once per switch, with the value that
switch is to have. \texttt{-bf} makes a trace the shortest and makes the
reported depth the breadth-first level. \texttt{-nodead} is needed because a
slice with the operations that re-register a session removed deadlocks when
every roster is gone, which is not a violation. The invariant schemas are
deleted only because they are not operations and the goals below replace
them. By the convention of \S\ref{sec:lc-method} every state predicate is a
\texttt{-goal}; the \texttt{GOAL}s, in B, are the negations of the invariant
schemas:

to keep the clause as defensive code and to say so. The resume path of the
reconcile carries the supersede clause (it closes the fork skeleton the
child's binding names when that is not the record's). No reachable state

\subsection{Consequences for the design document}
\label{sec:lc-consequences}

With every switch on, the operations of this section are the authority.
DES-078 is to be brought into line at these places.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \S2.1.1, \field{ensure} rule~1 and \S2.3.1: add the cycle refusal of
    C3. The sentence ``a cycle cannot be written through \field{ensure}
    except through a \field{--parent-gone} parent'' is false and goes.
  \item \S2.1.1 and \S2.6, \S5 point~1, \field{deleteFiles}: the order is
    close named skeletons, active-mission claim, delegation binding,
    pending record, last-seen, roster, then \fpath{parent-session} last
    (C2, C5). \field{Repair} is unchanged and now covers a failure at the
    last step.
  \item \S5 point~2 item~2 and item~5: a refusal reconciles (C1); the claimed
    case closes the skeleton unless the binding is live, which replaces the
    round-7 paragraph on ``the record and the binding name the same
    delegation'' (C4); the mission is re-read once the skeleton is on disk
    (C6).
  \item \S2.1.1, \field{isStale} and its test table: row~2 becomes not
    stale (C7, a decision of the leader recorded in
    \S\ref{sec:lc-ledger}); an unreadable roster with a fresh last-seen is
    not purged (C8); a test case for each.
  \item \S2.1.1, \field{deleteFiles} step~1, \S2.6, \S5 point~2 item~5 and the
    CLI \field{mission release} (C10, C11): every close closes only an open
    skeleton of kind \field{dkFork} that the session owns. In the code the
    kind is the skeleton's \field{BoundVia}: \field{claim-forked} writes a new
    value (for example \texttt{claim\_forked}), the close routine loads the
    record and checks \field{BoundVia} and \field{ParentSession} before it
    closes, and a Tier B binding is cleared but its skeleton is left. The
    prose that says step~1 closes ``every open skeleton named by the
    session's \ldots binding'' becomes ``every open fork-propagated skeleton
    that the session owns and that its record or binding names''.
    \field{runMissionRelease} (\fpath{cmd/ethos/mission.go}:2135--2179) gains
    the same close, before it clears the binding; that is a change to an
    existing Claude Code command and belongs to the implementation PR.
  \item \S5 point~2 item~5, the paragraph on C9: the wording ``a reconcile
    that does not resume the record'' is withdrawn; the clause applies on the
    two write-proceeding reconcile paths (fresh and resume), with or without a
    record, and never on a refusal or the claimed case. The trace is no longer
    ``completed propagation, release, second propagation'', since release
    clears the binding; it is a close-time clearing of another mission
    (\texttt{C9\_nosup}).
  \item \S2.1.1 (``Existing creators respect the sidecar'') and \S2.5 (C13):
    \field{SessionStart} for \emph{every} source calls \field{Store.Ensure}
    without \field{--touch}, in process; the sources other than \field{fork}
    pass the parent \field{ParentOf} records. The sentence that a
    \field{SessionStart} for any other source keeps today's
    \field{CreateInCheckout} path is false of a resumed or compacted fork and
    goes. A required test: a \field{SessionStart} with \field{source}
    \texttt{resume}, and one with \texttt{compact}, against a fork that has a
    roster and a sidecar, change neither.
  \item \S5 point~2 item~5 and \fpath{mission/store.go}:1107--1114,
    \fpath{mission/delegation.go}:649--692 (C14): every skeleton close is a
    compare-and-set on verdict \field{open} under the delegation lock, and
    \field{closeDelegationSkeletons}, in the locked and the unlocked branch
    alike, takes the delegation lock of each skeleton it closes. Today
    \field{CloseDelegationSkeleton} rewrites the verdict it is given.
  \item \S5 point~2 write phase, steps~2 to 7 (C15): on the resume path the
    reconcile of step~2 is followed by steps~5 and~6 (the mission and
    delegation locks, the re-read of the mission) before the binding and the
    claim are written, exactly as on the fresh path; a mission found closed
    exits ``do not retry''. The statement that the resume ``finishes the
    missing writes'' omits the locks.
  \item \S5 point~2 item~5 and \S2.1.1 (C12, the leader's ruling): the
    \field{claim-forked-pending} record is \emph{not} removed as the last step
    of a successful write. It stays, marked complete, for the life of the
    claim, and is the durable name of the fork skeleton. It is removed only
    with the skeleton it names: \field{mission release}, the close-time
    clearing of the claim (\field{ClearMissionBindings}, which today clears
    the claim and the binding and must also close and unname the
    record-named skeleton) and \field{deleteFiles} (step~4, after step~1 has
    closed it). The sentences ``removed as the last step of a successful
    write'' and ``the record is then overwritten or removed'' change
    accordingly; the dispatch path of \fpath{pretooluse\_dispatch.go} is not
    changed. Reconcile must distinguish the two lives of a record: a
    complete record is never resumed and is live when the child's claim is
    for the skeleton's mission, whatever the binding names; an incomplete one
    keeps the rule of C4. The record needs a stage field (complete or not)
    that the YAML of today does not have, and the "record does not parse"
    row of the exit-status table applies to it for as long as the claim
    lasts.

Further consequences are not counterexamples of the model but
conditions under which its amended operations are the ones the code runs.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{N2. The close step of \field{deleteFiles} short-circuits.}
    \field{fixDeleteCloses} places the close at step~1 and every later step
    assumes it succeeded: \field{LcDeleteSession} clears the claim, the
    binding and the pending record only after the skeletons are closed. A
    failed close must therefore end the call there, with nothing cleared and
    the roster kept as the retry token. This is unlike
    \field{clearMissionSidecarsLocked} (\fpath{session/store.go}:336--348),
    which joins its errors and continues; a copy of that shape would clear
    the only names the open skeleton has and reinstate C5.
  \item \textbf{N4. \field{Acyclic} depends on serialised edge insertion, or
    on the visited set.} \field{fixCycleCheck} refuses an edge that would
    close a cycle by reading the recorded chain; \field{Ensure} holds only
    the lock of the session it writes. Two sessions inserting the two edges
    of a cycle concurrently each read a chain without the other's edge, and
    both succeed. The model's operations are atomic and cannot show this.
    Either edge insertion is serialised across sessions, or the visited set
    of the walk of \S2.3.1 is mandatory and the walk refuses on a repeat,
    which is what the design says it does; the statement that the visited
    set is ``a defence only'' holds of a serialised store and no other.
  \item \textbf{N5. Every close other than the sweep writes verdict
    \field{aborted}, and none changes a verdict.} \field{countBlockingDelegations}
    (\fpath{mission/store.go}:2004--2010) counts every delegation that is
    neither \field{aborted} nor disclaimed. The closes that
    \field{fixDeleteCloses}, \field{fixRefuseReconciles},
    \field{fixInertBinding}, \field{fixSupersede} and \field{LcCfAbort} add
    all remove a skeleton from \field{dOpen}; in the code each must write
    verdict \field{aborted}, or the skeleton stops being open in the model
    and still blocks \field{Abandon} in the code. The model has the bit
    open or not and, since C14, the bit aborted or not; it has no other
    verdict, and the sweep's stands for pass, fail and error alike. Each
    close site is to be tested for the verdict it writes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this model does not establish}
\label{sec:lc-limits}

Each operation is one atomic step, as in the rest of this document, except
that a \field{claim-forked} write phase and a \field{deleteFiles} are
broken at the points where a process can die or a removal can fail. The
locks are therefore assumed to do what the Non-goals section of \texttt{spec-opencode-integration.tex} says; two
exceptions are modelled, the shared mission lock that excludes a locked
sweep and the absence of any exclusion under the unlocked fallback. The
read phase and the start of the write phase are one step: the window in
which the witness releases its claim between them is accepted by the
design and not examined. A read-phase refusal and the unlocked fallback
are always enabled, which over-approximates the real conditions. The claim
\field{claim-forked} writes for the child is given claim origin; the design
does not say. Three limits of the code are not represented. (N3) \field{Store.Close}'s
sweep skips a delegation record it cannot load and only logs a failure to
close one (\fpath{mission/store.go}:1236, 1242--1244), so a skeleton the
sweep misses is not an error anywhere; and a skeleton directory with no
\fpath{record.yaml} makes \field{countBlockingDelegations} return an error
(\fpath{mission/store.go}:1994--2000), which refuses \field{Abandon} for a
reason the model has no state for. (N7) The origin of a binding that is
unknown, and the fact that the claim \field{claim-forked} writes for the
child is two files, the active-mission sidecar and its origin, are not
represented: \field{claimed} and \field{corigin} are written by one step, and
\field{BORIGIN} has two values. (N8) \field{NoLiveSessionPurged} judges
liveness only from rosters it can read (\S\ref{sec:lc-invariants}). A live
Claude Code session whose roster fails to parse and which has no valid
last-seen is still purged, as it is today; \field{fixCorruptPurge} protects
only an unreadable roster that carries a fresh last-seen, and the invariant
says nothing of the other. (N9) The Tier B dispatch is one atomic
step; its own re-read of the mission and the unlocked fallback can pass each
other as C6 shows for \field{claim-forked}, and the model does not show it
for Tier B. (N10) The compare-and-set of C14 is taken to be atomic, as the
delegation lock is; the model shows the sweep's write racing a reconcile's
close and not the reverse interleaving, which the same lock and compare
exclude, and a reconcile's close is one step. (N11) Under
\field{fixSessionStart} a \field{SessionStart} for a roster that exists
but does not parse is a no-op, where \field{CreateInCheckout} rewrites it;
the purge reclaims it, and whether the hook should heal it is not decided
here. (N12) The owner test of the origin filter is vacuous in the model,
whose only writers of a binding are the session itself; it is kept because
the amendment is stated in terms of it.
(N16) The code's \field{mission release}
(\fpath{cmd/ethos/mission.go}:2135--2179) and the close-time clearing
(\fpath{internal/mission/active.go}:907--957) clear the claim and the binding
without a lock, and release is idempotent when no claim exists.
\field{LcReleaseClaim} and \field{LcClearClosed} are atomic, are taken under
the lock, and require a claim. The implementation must hold the child's
dispatch-pending lock around the whole close, unname, clear sequence, or the
closes of C11 and C12 can race a write phase, and the model cannot show it.
(N17) Three omissions in the Tier B dispatch: a dispatch without a roster, the
depth-gate close with verdict \field{aborted}, and a binding write that fails
without being fatal are not represented. (N18) That a release removes the
record is unverified: \texttt{MyRelKeepRec} survives, so it is hygiene and not
an invariant. The same holds of the guard $c\notin\field{pendDone}$ of
\field{LcCfReconcileReuse}, stated above (\texttt{MutReuseDone}).
(N13) \field{ClearMissionBindings} is modelled for the closing session's
claim and binding only; the dispatch-pending entries it clears, and the MCP
path that calls it, are not represented. (N15) The record's stage is one bit, complete or not; the record
of the code also carries the delegation, mission and witness, which the model
keeps in \field{pend}, \field{cfM} and \field{pendWit}, and the mission of a
complete record is not re-checked against the claim except through
\field{live}. A record that no longer parses while the claim lasts is outside
the model (N3). (N14) \field{LcSessionStart} for
the sources \field{startup} and \field{clear} adds nothing to
\field{resume}: both name an id that has no roster unless it was registered
by another channel. Time is three values with a threshold of one, and the
participant kinds are three; larger values add no behaviour to
\field{isStale}, which compares only. The instance has three sessions for
the lineage and purge slices and two for the \field{claim-forked} slices
(\S\ref{sec:lc-verification}): the claim slices were also run at three,
bounded, and those runs are marked by their state count.

\subsection{Findings about the toolchain}
\label{sec:lc-findings}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The predicate part of a state schema becomes a guard of every
    operation in \texttt{probcli}'s translation (\S\ref{sec:lc-method}). The
    ``zero invariant violations'' of the Validation section of
    \texttt{spec-opencode-integration.tex} for \field{Registry} are claims
    about a machine in which a violating transition cannot occur; they say
    nothing about whether one is possible. This does not affect the
    \field{Registry} operations' guards, which are the stated protocol, but
    it does affect the word ``invariant''. The state predicates of
    \field{Registry} should be re-checked as goals.
  \item A document is one machine. An earlier revision of this model sat
    inside \texttt{spec-opencode-integration.tex}; \texttt{/z-spec:test} of
    that file then promoted every schema of the model to an operation of
    \field{Registry}, and the setsize~1 run rose from 286 states and 3862
    transitions, certified complete, to 14692 transitions and no
    certificate. With the model in this file the Registry run is again
    286 states, 3862 transitions, deadlock-free, with
    \field{ForkSession} and \field{AllocateDelegationForkPropagated} the
    only uncovered operations, for the reason that document gives.
\end{itemize}
